<p><strong>Allocation Criteria for Subjection Combinations</strong>
</p>
<ol>
<li>
<p><em>Merit</em>
</p>
</li>
</ol>
<p>The primary consideration is the student’s academic performance. Students
who have done well overall, and who meet the prerequisites for their preferred
subject combination, will be given priority.</p>
<p>
<br>
</p>
<ol start="2">
<li>
<p><em>Choice Order</em>
</p>
</li>
</ol>
<p>The school values each student’s interests and preferences. Students will
be allocated the subject combination in the order of their choice, provided
they meet all the pre-requisite criteria.</p>
<p>
<br>
</p>
<ol start="3">
<li>
<p><em>Availability of Places</em>
</p>
</li>
</ol>
<p>While the school will make every effort to accommodate students’ choices,
the final allocation is also subject to the availability of resources and
teaching manpower. In instances where the subject combination has reached
its maximum enrolment size*, students will be allocated to their next choice.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p>
<p>
<br><em>* Elective subjects such as Computing, Nutrition and Food Science (NFS), Design and Technology (D&amp;T), and Music have limited vacancies as they require the use of specialised facilities.</em>
</p>
<p></p>
<p>
<br>
</p>
<p><strong>Common Academic Requirement</strong>
</p>
<p>Students to attain subject overall mark of at least 50% (regardless of
subject level) in:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>English Language and 2 subjects, or</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>At least half the total number of examinable subjects offered</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>
<br>
</p>
<p><strong>Full Subject-Based Banding</strong>
</p>
<p>At the end of Sec 2 and Sec 3, students must meet the common academic
requirement to offer subjects at the same subject levels the following
year. Students who have not met academic requirements are to calibrate
their curricular load by one or more of the following ways:</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Reducing the number of subjects (e.g. only offering 6 subjects)</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Offering <s>elective</s> subject(s) at Less Demanding Level (e.g. G3 to
G2)</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><s>Offering compulsory subjects at Less Demanding Level if the student obtained E8 or below at G3, or 6 at G2.</s>
</p>
</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Polytechnic Year 1: Aggregate Computation</strong>
</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>From the AY2028 intake, for the ELR2B2 admission criteria, students can
offer one [B] subject taken at either G2 or G3. The remaining four subjects
must continue to be offered at G3 to ensure that students can cope with
the academic rigour in the polytechnics.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Students will be assessed on a common benchmark of four G3 subjects and
one G2 subject. Students who offer both [B] subjects at G3 will have their
[B] subject with a lower grade mapped from G3 to G2 based on an empirically
validated downward grade mapping table.&nbsp;</p>
</li>

raw GPA of 3.5 will be guaranteed a place in a mapped polytechnic course.</p>

Year 2 for all Higher Nitec courses will be set at G2.</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Students taking a combination of G3 and G2 subjects can apply for direct
entry into Year 2 of Higher Nitec, as long as they obtain ELMAB3≤ 19. G3
subjects will be mapped to G2 for the computation of the aggregate score.</p>

<p><strong>Frequently Asked Questions</strong>
</p>
<p>1 <em>Can my son opt to take 8 or more subjects at upper secondary?</em>
</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>A 6 to 7 subject combination allows students time to learn and understand
the subjects offered in greater depth.&nbsp;</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Also enables them to have time for non-academic pursuits and develop their
passion (e.g. co-curricular areas) so that they have a more holistic school
experience.&nbsp;</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>The time spent in developing your passion can contribute to your EAE application</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Admission to Junior College (JC) and Polytechnic, is based on 5 relevant
subjects only.</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>
<br>
</p>
<p>2 <em>If they perform well in a core subject by end Sec 3, can they move to G3 in Sec 4?</em>
</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>The increase in syllabus coverage and rigour from G2 to G3 is substantial.&nbsp;</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>The runway to adequately prepare the student to switch from G2 to G3 is
limited and this can be very stressful.</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>
<br>
</p>
<p>3 <em>During Sec 3, what happens if students can’t handle the subjects as well as expected? (G3-&gt;G2)</em>
</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>This will be reviewed on a case-by-case basis.&nbsp;</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>The school can provide counselling and work with your son to strategise
the best subject combination to maximise his chance of attaining his goals.</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>
<br>
</p>
<p>4 <em>Do students have the option of a mix of G2 and G3 subjects?</em>
</p>
<p><em>Can a G2 student choose any of the G3 subject combinations?</em>
</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>Yes, please book an appointment to discuss the subject combination in
October after release of results</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>
<br>
</p>
<p>5 <em>Can we offer triple science? Do we need triple science to go JC?</em>
</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p>A student does not need to take Triple Science in order to pursue a science-related
course at JC.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>For JC science courses, students must offer Mathematics, up to two Science
subjects, and a contrasting Humanities subject.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Different JCs may have their own specific requirements and considerations.
These are published on the respective JC websites.</p>
</li>
</ul>
